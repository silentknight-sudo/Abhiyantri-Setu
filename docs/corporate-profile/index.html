Deliverable 10 · Executive summary

Abhiyantri Setu in one page

A digital platform that connects everyone who builds, repairs, supplies and maintains property in India: homeowners, businesses, contractors, tradespeople, engineers, suppliers and equipment owners.

Who we are

Abhiyantri Setu Digital Infra Pvt. Ltd., a ConTech company founded by Avinish Tripathi in Greater Noida. Confirmed

Why we exist

Construction in India is large and fragmented. Customers struggle to find reliable contractors, compare honest quotations and procure material; capable providers struggle to be found.

What the platform does

Customers post a requirement, receive multiple quotations from verified providers, compare, hire, track work and rate it. The same rails later carry material procurement, equipment rental, professional consultation and enterprise RFQs.

How we earn

Commissions on completed jobs, lead fees, vendor subscriptions, supply and rental margins, consultation fees and, later, enterprise procurement and AMC contracts. Proposed

What exists today

Company incorporated, GST registered, brand identity, social accounts and a working web application codebase (Next.js, PostgreSQL) with client job posting, provider bidding and dashboards. Historical / Codebase confirmed

What is pending

Compliance verification, PRD sign-off, production launch, vendor onboarding at scale, payments, SOPs, and the first paying customers in Greater Noida.

How we grow

Win one micro-market (Greater Noida) with Build & Renovate and Quick Home Services, then add materials and rentals, expand across NCR (2027–29), then selected metros (2029–31).

Where we want to be by 2035

A nationally trusted ecosystem for the full construction lifecycle: discovery, procurement, execution and maintenance, for households and enterprises.

One Platform. The Entire Construction Ecosystem.

Our story

Anyone who has built or renovated a home in Greater Noida knows the routine: calling contacts for a mason, getting three verbal quotes that cannot be compared, buying cement from whoever answers the phone, and chasing a contractor who has moved on to the next site.

Abhiyantri Setu began from that experience. The name joins Abhiyantri (engineering) with Setu (bridge): a bridge between people who need building work done and the people who do it well. The company chose to start where it can see every job, in one city, before trying to serve a country.

Part B · 09–10

Our solution & positioning

Positioning: India's Digital Construction & Infrastructure Ecosystem. Not a home-services app with a construction tab, but one record that follows a property from design to build to maintenance.

Discover

Verified profiles of contractors, trades, professionals, suppliers and equipment owners, by location and category.

Compare

Multiple structured quotations against one requirement, line by line.

Procure & execute

Materials and equipment sourced through the same project; milestones and photos tracked.

Maintain

AMC, repairs and facility services linked to the same property record.

Lifecycle advantage (proposed): each completed project generates data (scope, rates, materials, vendor performance) that improves matching, pricing and cross-sell for the next stage. This is the intended moat; it exists only once transaction volume does.

Part B · 11–12

The ecosystem & six business verticals

VERTICAL 01 · MVP launch

Build & Renovate

Scope: new home, residential and commercial construction; renovation; waterproofing; painting; flooring and tiling; interior fit-out; POP, gypsum, PVC/WPC; boundary walls and gates; roofing and sheds; fabrication; masonry and civil; structural engineering; labour contractors.

Flow: customer posts scope + photos/drawings → site visit by shortlisted contractors (for jobs above a threshold) → itemised quotes on a standard BOQ template → hire → milestone plan → stage photos and QC checklist → handover and warranty note.

Vendors: KYC, GST/PAN, 3+ past project photos, references; structural work requires a qualified engineer.

Money: 3–6% success commission on contract value, or lead fee for small jobs Proposed.

VERTICAL 02 · MVP launch

Quick Home Services

Scope: mason, painter, carpenter, plumber, electrician, tile fitter, deep cleaning, home maintenance, AC/HVAC, repairs, AMCs.

Flow: pick service → describe issue, slot and address → request routed to nearest available verified provider (or 2–3 quotes for non-standard jobs) → visit, inspection charge or rate card → completion OTP → rating.

Money: 10–20% commission on service value, AMC margins Proposed.

VERTICAL 03 · Phase 2

Material Marketplace

Scope: cement, TMT steel, sand, aggregates, bricks and blocks, tiles, marble/granite, paints, sanitaryware, electrical, plumbing, chemicals, interior and hardware.

Flow: supplier lists catalogue and delivery radius → customer or contractor raises an RFQ (brand, grade, quantity, site) → suppliers quote incl. freight → confirm, advance/COD → dispatch tracking → delivery proof.

Money: 1–4% supplier commission (commodity) to 5–10% (finishes) Proposed. Asset-light first; no inventory.

VERTICAL 04 · Phase 2

Equipment Rental

Scope: excavators, cranes, scaffolding, cuplock, shuttering, mixers, vibrators, lifting equipment, power tools, industrial equipment.

Key terms: rental period (hour/day/month), transport both ways, security deposit, damage and loss policy, operator inclusion and licence, fuel, minimum billing.

Money: 8–15% booking commission Proposed.

VERTICAL 05 · Phase 1–2

Find Professionals

Scope: architects, civil and structural engineers, interior designers, MEP, electrical, plumbing and HVAC consultants, PMC, quantity surveyors, safety, Vastu, engineering consultants.

Profiles: qualifications (CoA registration for architects), portfolio, service area, fee basis. Engagement: paid consultation, site visit, or project fee.

Money: 10–15% on booked consultations; subscriptions for visibility Proposed.

VERTICAL 06 · Strategic expansion

Business & Infrastructure Solutions

Scope: STP/ETP/WTP, RO plants, fire fighting, HVAC and pumping, hydropneumatic systems, industrial pumps, VFD panels, automation, rainwater harvesting, borewells, commercial and institutional projects, facility management, industrial maintenance, EPC services.

Why it matters: fewer, larger tickets; recurring AMC and FM revenue; requires technical pre-sales and bid management.

Money: 2–5% sourcing fee on project value; AMC/FM margins Proposed.

Part B · 13–17 · Deliverable 3

How the platform works

The core object is the Requirement (a job in the current codebase). Every participant either creates requirements, responds to them, or supplies what fulfils them. The admin team sits in the middle and guarantees process, not outcome.

A · Customer journey

  1. Visits website or app
  2. Selects category
  3. Submits requirement
  4. Enters location and project details
  5. Uploads photos, drawings, documents
  6. Requirement reaches platform; ops qualifies it
  7. Platform identifies 3–5 suitable providers
  8. Providers submit quotations
  9. Customer compares on one screen
  10. Selects provider
  11. Scope and commercial terms confirmed in writing
  12. Execution begins
  13. Progress updates and stage photos
  14. Completion sign-off
  15. Feedback and rating
  16. After-service and warranty support

B–C · Vendor and contractor journey

  1. Registration (mobile OTP)
  2. Profile creation
  3. KYC and business verification
  4. Category selection
  5. Service area (pin codes / radius)
  6. Portfolio and experience
  7. Verification and approval
  8. Lead notifications
  9. Quotation submission
  10. Customer communication
  11. Project confirmation
  12. Work execution
  13. Completion documentation
  14. Payment and commission settlement
  15. Performance monitoring

Codebase fit: the existing provider profile already tracks onboarding steps (basic info, services and pricing, work photos, ID verification, bank details) and a verified flag. Bids carry status, lead status, progress and schedule. Steps 1–12 have a data foundation; settlement and performance monitoring do not yet.

D · Material supplier journey

  1. Supplier registration and GST check
  2. Catalogue with brand, grade, unit, specs
  3. Delivery coverage and minimum order
  4. Indicative pricing; stock status
  5. Receives RFQs and bulk enquiries
  6. Quotes incl. freight and lead time
  7. Purchase confirmation and advance
  8. Logistics and delivery proof
  9. Order closure and invoice

E · Equipment rental provider

  1. Register fleet with photos, capacity, year
  2. Set rates, minimum billing, deposit
  3. Availability calendar
  4. Receive booking request
  5. Confirm operator, transport, fuel terms
  6. Handover checklist with photos
  7. Return inspection; damage assessment
  8. Deposit release and commission settlement

F · Professional consultant

Profile with registration and qualifications → portfolio → fee basis (per visit, per sq ft, lump sum) → consultation booking → deliverables uploaded (drawings, reports) → rating.

G · Enterprise journey

  1. Business account with GSTIN, multiple users
  2. Project requirement submission
  3. RFQ generated with technical spec
  4. Vendor shortlisting (pre-qualified)
  5. Technical and commercial bid comparison
  6. Negotiation
  7. PO or contract documentation
  8. Project milestones
  9. Delivery and quality tracking
  10. Invoice and payment management

H · Platform administrator

Verifies vendors, qualifies requirements, routes leads, configures categories and commissions, monitors SLAs, handles disputes, refunds and fraud signals, and reports weekly metrics.

Technology architecture

Important finding: earlier planning favoured Firebase. The current codebase is built on Next.js (React, TypeScript), Prisma ORM and PostgreSQL, with session/account tables consistent with a standard auth library. This document recommends continuing on that stack rather than migrating to Firebase. Founder and developers should confirm.

BackendNext.js API routes, server actionsSplit matching, payments and notifications into services when teams grow

Firebase assessment

Firebase suits a fast prototype (auth, push, hosting). For a quote-and-transaction marketplace with relational data (jobs, bids, payments, commissions, reports), PostgreSQL is the better fit and is already in use. Firebase Cloud Messaging can still be used for push notifications.

Future technology (all proposed; none exists today)

AI requirement classification from text and photos · vendor matching on distance, rating and price fit · quote anomaly detection · construction cost estimator from area and specification · photo-based progress tracking · digital procurement for contractors · predictive maintenance for AMC assets · enterprise ERP integrations.

Break-even logic: fixed opex ÷ (take rate × contribution margin %) = GMV needed. At ₹110 L opex, a 7% take rate and 70% contribution margin on revenue, break-even GMV ≈ ₹22 crore a year (~₹1.9 crore a month). Cash flow: keep 12–18 months of runway; avoid inventory and supplier credit until working-capital financing is arranged; collect commission at payment time rather than invoicing vendors later.

  • Completion sign-off by OTP

Payments & refunds

  • Fees disclosed upfront
  • Milestone payments for projects
  • Cancellation and refund policy per category
  • Invoices for every transaction

Complaints & disputes

  • Acknowledge in 24 h, resolve plan in 72 h
  • Re-visit or rework first, refund second
  • Grievance officer per consumer e-commerce rules
  • Escalation to mediation for large disputes

Data & security

  • Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 consent and notices
  • Minimal storage of ID documents, encrypted
  • Role-based admin access, audit logs
  • Backups and incident response plan

Vendor performance

  • Scorecard: rating, response time, punctuality, rework, complaints
  • Warnings → suspension → delisting

Strategic roadmap 2026–2035

Budgets are indicative planning estimates, not company-approved budgets. Each phase starts only when the previous phase's exit criteria are met.

Phase 1Nov 2026 – Dec 2027

Foundation & validation · Greater Noida

Objective: prove customers will post, vendors will quote and both will pay. Activities: PRD, MVP, vendor onboarding, Build & Renovate and Quick Home Services launch, SOPs, first revenue. Dependencies: compliance, developer capacity, seed capital. KPIs: 300 verified vendors, 3+ quotes per requirement, 25% requirement-to-hire, NPS > 40. Team: 5–8. Budget: ₹60 L–1.2 Cr. Risks: thin supply, disintermediation. Exit: 150+ paid jobs/month, positive contribution on home services.

Phase 22027 – 2029

NCR expansion

Noida → Ghaziabad → Delhi → Gurugram and Faridabad. Launch material RFQs and equipment rental, full professional discovery, first B2B and AMC contracts. KPIs: 5 cities live, ₹25 Cr+ annual GMV, 20% repeat rate. Team: 20–40. Budget: ₹5–15 Cr. Risks: city playbook not repeatable, cash burn. Exit: each city reaches contribution break-even within 9 months.

Phase 32029 – 2031

National expansion

Enter 3–5 metros (e.g. Lucknow, Jaipur, Pune, Bengaluru, Hyderabad) chosen by data. City teams, enterprise partnerships, industrial services, standardised tech and ops. Budget: ₹30–80 Cr. Exit: company-level EBITDA break-even path visible.

Phase 42031 – 2033

Ecosystem integration

Advanced project management, integrated procurement, enterprise dashboards, marketplace intelligence and automation, financing partnerships.

Phase 52033 – 2035

Industry leadership

Nationwide ecosystem, strategic industry partnerships, technology services to developers and contractors, large enterprise integrations; evaluate international (e.g. GCC, South Asia) opportunities.
